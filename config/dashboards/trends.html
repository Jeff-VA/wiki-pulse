The news classifier

Trained on {samples} bursts Claude labeled, {news_share} of them news. It calls news from a probability of {threshold}, which on bursts it hadn't trained on caught {threshold_recall} of news, {threshold_precision} of its calls right. Last trained {trained_at} UTC; it retrains hourly.

Why a classifier, when Claude reads bursts? Claude reads fresh bursts within a daily budget, a few seconds after each and at a cost. A logistic regression trained on its labels scores every burst the moment it's flagged, for free: Claude's judgment, distilled into eight numbers.

How it learns. A pipeline step refits it hourly on every burst Claude has labeled, chooses the probability at which it calls news (the most news caught at 50% precision, on articles it didn't train on), and publishes both as a table, behind a quality gate: its average precision must close at least a fifth of the gap between random guessing and perfect. The burst detector reloads them every 15 minutes.

How it's checked. Agreement with Claude, above, counts only bursts scored before Claude labeled them, so it's accuracy on bursts the model hadn't seen.
